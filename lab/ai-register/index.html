Agent Register

An AI governance prototype that helps teams describe their agents, establish ownership and give reviewers a clearer picture of how AI is being used across an organization.

Personal buildAI governanceWorkspace

What it is

Agent Register brings business context and technical implementation into a shared governance workflow. Submitters begin with a simple user story: who will use the agent, what it will help them accomplish and why that outcome matters.

The intake then explores the platform, data sources, actions and human oversight involved. Questions adapt to how the agent is being built, allowing business teams to explain its purpose and technical owners to document its implementation.

The governance view organizes agents by department and business owner, with drill-downs into teams, submission statuses and individual records. Reviewers can identify missing information, request changes and record decisions. Indicators such as external data sharing, write access and human approval requirements help focus attention without assigning an automatic risk rating.

Why I built it

I built Agent Register to explore how governance can become part of the way teams create and deploy AI.

A useful review starts with a shared understanding of purpose, ownership, data and authority. Business users should be able to explain what they need in familiar language. Technical teams should be able to supply implementation details without asking the business owner to guess.

This project turns that idea into a working prototype. It gives me a practical way to test intake questions, review workflows and organizational views, and to explore how governance can provide useful guidance as AI adoption grows.

How it was built

I developed the prototype iteratively with AI-assisted coding. I defined the governance questions, ownership structure and review workflow, then used AI to help implement the interface, conditional forms and supporting logic. Each iteration added a specific capability, from user-story intake to technical review and departmental drill-downs.

The application uses HTML, CSS and JavaScript and is hosted as a private site. Records currently persist in the user’s browser. The demonstration workspace contains 16 fictional agents across four departments, stored separately from other workspace records.

It is a working prototype for testing and feedback. Shared storage, authenticated roles and a production audit trail remain future work.
