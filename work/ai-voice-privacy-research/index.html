Case study

Research behind AI voice features: privacy, disclosure and adoption

Adopting voice-driven messaging meant a spectrum of AI features, privacy disclosures and cross-platform applications. I led research that shaped adoption and roadmap decisions across product, privacy, legal and compliance.

The situation

Voice messaging in VR for Facebook Messenger depended on speech-to-text and AI features. As part of the foundational research team, we focused on open questions around adoption and retention of voice users. The work improved the overall user experience by focusing on feature implementation and appropriate privacy messaging in disclaimer notices.

My role

I led the biannual research strategy across voice, speech-to-text, AI, AR/VR and smart communication experiences, partnering with product managers, designers, engineers, data scientists, and the Privacy, Legal and Compliance teams. I also coordinated governance for studies involving AI, voice and sensitive user data.

What I did

  • Tested disclosure with real users. Redesigned speech-to-text disclosures so they preserved regulatory intent while increasing user trust and first-time voice adoption.
  • Translated research into decisions. Connected findings to quarterly KPIs, roadmap priorities and longer-term strategy, and gave stakeholders direction on privacy and legal concerns.
  • Paired behavior with qualitative data to establish behavior-based benchmarks.
  • Made the work reusable. Contributed studies to research libraries; the findings became a reference for voice-based design in AR and wearables.

Outcome

Research informed 12 of 21 high-priority initiatives for the second half of 2022. A single UI finding the team had not previously identified increased new voice usage by an estimated 30% in internal testing. The disclosure redesign showed that disclaimer language mattered more than originally thought and could be a driver of adoption. The studies became foundational for multiple voice-based design implementations.
