Approach

Optimize then automate. Govern so teams can move faster.

The principle came from a Six Sigma engagement over twenty years ago and still holds for AI: fix the process, then add the technology, and keep the controls proportionate to the risk.

  1. Pilot one system or issue

    Start with one system or one issue. Run it through the process, see what works and what doesn’t, and fix it. What the team learns becomes the foundation for everything after, and their confidence is what lets them move faster.

  2. Adapt the governance

    Every organization operates differently and values different things, so governance has to fit. Many controls and goals are common across companies, but how they are applied is not. Adaptive governance starts from how your organization actually works.

  3. Expand gradually

    Each success and each lesson learned builds momentum. The team takes ownership of the work, makes decisions that fit the organization, and moves things forward with the right level of insight.

  4. Optimize then automate

    Once we have proven how the work gets done, built best practices and gained confidence in our decisions, automation follows naturally. Automating processes, applying AI and routing the right work to the right people speeds everything up, and the team experiences governance as an enabler.

Governance as enabler

Good governance is how teams say yes to AI with confidence. Lightweight, proportionate controls speed adoption because people know where the lines are.

Design to support adoption

Human-centered design drives adoption by making expectations clear to everyone: disclosures people can read, workflows that feel intuitive and controls that fit the context.
